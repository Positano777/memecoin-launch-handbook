# I - Identity, rights, and disclosures

Check that your name, image, domain, and social identity do not imply an affiliation that does not exist. Open-source dependencies retain their own licenses. Keep upstream notices and distinguish your original work from a fork, wrapper, tutorial, or hosted service.

Describe team holdings, compensation, developer purchases, creator fees, treasury control, retained authorities, and paid promotion. If revenue is intended to fund software, explain the route money takes and publish expenditure records. Avoid implying that token ownership grants equity, refunds, revenue rights, or governance powers that have not actually been established.

Legal and tax treatment depends on facts and jurisdiction. Write down the jurisdictions, intended audience, marketing claims, and money flows that require qualified review; this handbook does not resolve them or provide a legal classification.

**Deliverable:** [public disclosures](../templates/disclosures.md) and a record of unresolved review questions.
